# A New Rabbit Hole: AI and Cognitive Science

It's been a while since I rebranded my website. I thought I was going to consistently put effort into this blog, but life had other plans. Work got busy, and I also went through a bit of a low period for a while, so writing naturally slipped off my list.

I'm slowly getting back to a better balance and trying to make my life feel a little more active and exciting again. I knew it was time to write another entry here, but thinking about what to write about is never easy, especially when I haven't been focused on one specific topic lately.

The good thing is that I've been trying to start small, picking things off my to-do list one by one. And one of those things has been reading articles about AI and cognitive science.

About a month ago, I went through a bit of a career crisis. At some point, I noticed myself giving a task to AI almost immediately, doing a quick review of the result, and then putting up a PR. That made me lose some confidence in myself. I started worrying that I wasn't learning as much as I wanted to as an engineer, and that if I kept working this way, I might eventually stop enjoying the work itself.

After a few days of worrying, thinking, and talking things through, I decided to experiment with using AI more deliberately. And somewhere in that process, I came across a new term: **cognitive offloading**.

I've only read a handful of articles so far, but I especially enjoyed this one from the APA: https://www.apa.org/monitor/2026/07-08/ai-job-skills-thinking.

I don't really want to explain what the article is about just yet, since I've only recently started exploring this topic on the side. But I would love to occasionally share what I notice, feel, and think about as an engineer working in such a fast-moving industry.
